Drainage & Grading in Lehigh Acres, FL

Drainage and grading fix the standing water that flat Lehigh Acres lots are known for — reshaping the ground so rain flows away from the house instead of pooling against it. Residential grading typically runs $770–$3,000, and French drains run about $23–$35 per linear foot.

Why Lehigh Acres lots hold water

Lehigh Acres is flat — genuinely flat, with very little natural fall across most lots — and the sandy soil sits on a high water table. That combination means rain has almost nowhere to go. After a summer downpour, water ponds in low spots, creeps toward foundations, and turns yards into marsh for days. It is not a flaw in any one lot; it is what the ground does here. The fix is shaping the ground on purpose: a few inches of fall, in the right direction, over a long enough run, moves water that would otherwise sit still.

The other half of the problem is what sits under the sand. In parts of Lehigh Acres, a hard layer sits a foot or two below the surface and slows water from soaking down, so the top layer saturates fast in the wet season. A grading contractor who knows the area will check for that layer before promising that regrading alone will solve a wet yard — sometimes the answer is a drain, not just a slope.

What drainage and grading work includes

  • Regrading around the house — reshaping the ground so it falls away from the foundation on all sides, usually the first fix for water at the slab.
  • Swales — shallow, grassed channels cut across a lot to carry surface water toward the street, a ditch, or a low corner where it can drain away.
  • French drains — a gravel-filled trench with a perforated pipe that collects groundwater and surface water and carries it to a discharge point. The standard fix when the problem is water under the surface, not just on top of it.
  • Culverts and driveway drains — pipes under driveways and access points so water can keep moving instead of damming up at the edge of the pavement.
  • Retention and dry wells — a low area or buried chamber where runoff collects and soaks away slowly, used where there is no downhill outlet.

Drainage changes may need a permit

Regrading that changes how water leaves your lot — or sends it toward a neighbor's — can need a Lee County permit, and sending water onto someone else's property can create a liability problem. A reputable contractor will check the rules and the lot lines before cutting a swale or a drain. Ask whether permitting is included in the quote.

Grading vs. drains: which one fits your problem

Grading fixes water that sits on the surface. If the puddle forms on top of the ground after rain and drains away within a day, reshaping the slope usually solves it. Drains fix water that comes from below or won't move: a yard that stays soggy for days after rain, a low corner that never dries, or water seeping through a slab. Many Lehigh Acres jobs need both — regrading to move the surface water and a French drain to handle what soaks in. A contractor who only sells one of the two may be solving half your problem.

Questions to ask before you hire a drainage contractor

  • Is the problem surface water, groundwater, or both — and how did you tell?
  • Where will the water go once it's collected? Is there a legal discharge point?
  • Does the quote include regrading, the drain, and surface restoration — or just one of those?
  • Do you need a Lee County permit for this work, and is it in the price?
  • How do you handle Florida 811 locates before cutting swales or drain trenches?
  • What happens if the first fix doesn't solve it — is there a follow-up visit?

Local tip: watch your yard after a storm

The best time to diagnose a drainage problem is the morning after heavy rain. Take photos of where the water sits, how deep it is, and how long it takes to disappear — then share them with the contractor. A quote based on what the water actually does beats a quote based on a guess, and the photos help a pro tell you whether you need grading, a drain, or both.
