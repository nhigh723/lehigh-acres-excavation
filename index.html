Describe Your Project

Why digging in Lehigh Acres is different

Lehigh Acres sits on flat, sandy ground with groundwater close to the surface, and much of it is being built out right now — vacant lots platted decades ago are turning into new homes. That combination shapes every excavation job here in ways that surprise people who have only dug in other parts of the country:

  • The ground is easy to dig — and hard to keep open. Loose sand cuts fast with an excavator, which keeps digging costs down. But sandy trench walls cave in easily, so deeper trenches need sloping or shoring, and a hole left open overnight can slump. Speed on the dig is balanced by care on the walls.
  • Water shows up sooner than you'd expect. The water table is high across much of Lehigh Acres. A footing trench a few feet deep can start filling with groundwater, and anything deeper — a septic drainfield, a deep foundation, a pond — almost certainly will. Dewatering (pumping the hole dry while work goes on) is routine here, not a surprise, but it adds equipment and time to the quote.
  • Flat ground makes drainage the hard part. Lehigh Acres has very little natural slope, so water has nowhere to go on its own. A pad or a yard graded even slightly wrong will hold water after every summer storm. Getting the fall right — even a few inches over a long run — is what separates a good grading job from a future puddle.
  • New-build lots need pads and fill. Many Lehigh Acres lots sit low relative to the road and need imported fill to reach building elevation. Fill quantities — measured in cubic yards and truckloads — often cost as much as the digging itself, and a good quote breaks fill out as its own line.

Good to know before you get a quote

The four numbers that decide your price are depth, volume, water, and haul distance. A quote that doesn't ask how deep you're going, how much dirt moves, whether the hole will hit water, and where the spoil goes is guessing — and guesses get revised upward once the machine is on site.

Florida 811 comes first, every time

Florida law requires at least 2 full business days' notice before any excavation, so buried utilities can be located and marked. Your contractor usually handles the call — but confirm it is part of the job before any digging starts. Hitting an unmarked line is dangerous and expensive.

Common questions

How much does excavation cost in Lehigh Acres, FL?

Most residential excavation jobs run $1,500–$6,300, according to national cost guides, and digging alone typically costs $2.50–$15 per cubic yard. Footing trenches or a small pad usually run $1,500–$8,000, while a full foundation dig can reach $20,000–$75,000 or more. The final price depends on depth, volume, soil and water conditions, and hauling. See the full cost guide.

Do I need a permit for excavation in Lehigh Acres?

Lee County requires permits for most excavation tied to construction — foundations, septic systems, drainage changes, and demolition. Small standalone jobs such as a short utility trench may not need one. Your excavation contractor usually handles the permit as part of the job — ask whether permitting is included in the quote.

What does the high water table mean for digging in Lehigh Acres?

Groundwater sits close to the surface across much of Lehigh Acres, so deeper digs often hit water. When that happens the crew has to pump the water out — dewatering — to keep the excavation stable and workable. Dewatering adds time and cost, and it is the most common reason a Lehigh Acres excavation quote comes in higher than the per-yard rate alone would suggest.

How long does an excavation job take?

A short trench or a small pad usually takes a day or two. A foundation dig for a new home takes several days, and full site excavation can run a week or more depending on volume and weather. Wet-season rain (May–October) can pause work when the ground is saturated.
