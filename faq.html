Excavation FAQ — Lehigh Acres, FL

Answers to the questions Lehigh Acres homeowners ask most about digging — costs, permits, the water table, and how quotes work. Anything not covered here, ask in your quote request and a local pro will answer it.

How much does excavation cost in Lehigh Acres, FL?

Most residential excavation jobs run $1,500–$6,300, according to national cost guides, and digging alone typically costs $2.50–$15 per cubic yard. Footing trenches or a small pad usually run $1,500–$8,000, while a full foundation dig can reach $20,000–$75,000 or more. The final price depends on depth, volume, soil and water conditions, and hauling. See the full cost guide.

Do I need a permit for excavation in Lehigh Acres?

Lee County requires permits for most excavation tied to construction — foundations, septic systems, drainage changes, and demolition. Small standalone jobs such as a short utility trench may not need one. Your excavation contractor usually handles the permit as part of the job — ask whether permitting is included in the quote.

What is the water table problem in Lehigh Acres?

Groundwater sits close to the surface across much of Lehigh Acres, so deeper digs often hit water. When that happens the crew has to pump the water out — dewatering — to keep the excavation stable and workable. Dewatering adds time and cost, and it is the most common reason a Lehigh Acres excavation quote comes in higher than the per-yard rate alone would suggest.

What is Florida 811, and why does it matter for excavation?

Florida 811 is the free statewide service that marks buried utilities before anyone breaks ground. Florida law requires at least 2 full business days' notice before excavation so buried electric, water, gas, and telecom lines can be marked. Your contractor usually handles the locate request — confirm it is part of the job before any digging starts.

How is excavation priced — per job, per yard, or per hour?

All three are used. Defined projects such as a foundation dig or a driveway removal are usually priced per job. Large earthmoving is often priced per cubic yard of material moved. Small or open-ended jobs are often billed hourly, with machine and operator typically running $350–$450 per hour. Ask which method a quote uses before comparing it with another.

How long does excavation take?

A short trench or a small pad usually takes a day or two. A foundation dig for a new home takes several days, and full site excavation can run a week or more depending on volume and weather. Wet-season rain (May–October) can pause work when the ground is saturated.

What is the difference between excavation and grading?

Excavation removes earth — cutting trenches, pits, and pads, and moving material off or around the lot. Grading shapes the surface that remains — setting slopes so water drains away from the house and the ground is level where it needs to be. Most projects need both: excavation first to move the volume, grading last to finish the surface.

Can excavation happen during the wet season?

Yes, but wet-season work (May–October) can be slower and more expensive. Heavy rain saturates Lehigh Acres' sandy soil, fills open trenches with water, and can push jobs back days at a time. The dry season is usually faster and cleaner for digging, but a good operator works year-round and will tell you honestly if conditions need a wait.

What happens to the dirt that gets dug out?

Spoil is either reused on the lot — spread, used as fill, or stockpiled — or hauled away. Reusing it on site costs nothing extra. Hauling and disposal typically add $8–$25 per cubic yard on top of the digging. On a big dig, hauling can cost more than the digging itself, so ask whether a quote includes it.

Do I need dewatering for my project?

It depends on how deep you are digging and how high the water table is on your lot. Shallow trenches and pads often stay dry. Footing trenches a few feet deep can start seeping, and anything deeper — a septic drainfield, a deep foundation, a pond — will very likely hit water. Your contractor can tell from a site visit and a test hole whether dewatering will be needed.

Is rock a problem when digging in Lehigh Acres?

Rock is uncommon in Lehigh Acres' sandy soil, but limestone pockets exist in Southwest Florida. Rock that needs breaking out with a hydraulic hammer can cost $50–$200 per cubic yard, far more than digging sand. A good quote will say how rock is handled and priced if it shows up, rather than leaving it as an open-ended extra.

What should an excavation quote include?

A complete quote covers the digging, hauling and disposal (or reuse on site), fill quantities if the lot needs raising, dewatering if the dig is likely to hit water, permits, Florida 811 locates, backfill and compaction for trenches, and surface restoration. If any of those are missing, ask whether they are included or billed separately.

How deep can you dig before hitting water in Lehigh Acres?

It varies by lot and by season — the water table rises in the wet season and falls in the dry season. As a rough guide, shallow work under two feet usually stays dry, footing trenches at three to four feet often start seeping, and anything deeper should plan on dewatering. A test hole on your lot is the only way to know for sure.
