Trenching & Utilities in Lehigh Acres, FL

Trenching cuts the narrow channels that carry water, sewer, power, and gas to a home — dug to the right depth, around the lines already in the ground, then backfilled so the surface holds up. Basic trenching often runs $5–$12 per linear foot, with deeper utility runs costing more.

What trenching covers

Almost every utility on a Lehigh Acres lot arrives underground, and each one needs its own trench. Water lines run from the meter or well to the house. Sewer lines run from the house to the street connection or the septic system. Electric conduit carries power from the service point to the house, a workshop, or a gate. Gas lines, irrigation mains, and downspout drains each get their own run. The trench itself is only half the job — bedding the pipe in sand or gravel, laying it at the right slope, backfilling in layers, and compacting each layer so the ground above doesn't sink later are what make the difference between a trench that lasts and one that becomes a sunken line across the yard.

Depth depends on what's going in. A shallow irrigation or conduit trench might be a foot to eighteen inches deep. A water line typically sits two and a half to three feet down. A sewer line runs deeper still, and it has to hold a steady downhill slope the whole way — a sewer trench that goes flat or bellies will back up. That slope requirement is why sewer trenching is the most exacting of the bunch: the bottom of the trench has to be graded like a tiny road, not just dug.

Locates, depth, and sandy walls

Trenching in Lehigh Acres starts with Florida 811. Florida law requires at least 2 full business days' notice before any excavation, and the locate marks show where existing electric, water, gas, and telecom lines already run. A trench that crosses an unmarked line can cut power to a street or rupture a gas line — which is why no reputable operator digs before the marks are down. Your contractor usually handles the call; confirm it is part of the job before work starts.

The second local factor is the sand. Lehigh Acres' loose sandy soil digs fast, but trench walls in sand cave in easily — especially when the trench is deep or the ground is wet. Deeper trenches need their walls sloped back or shored, which takes more room and more time than a narrow cut. And because the water table is high, a deep trench can start filling with groundwater, which means pumping while the pipe is laid. All three — locates, wall stability, and water — are normal here, and all three belong in the quote.

Backfill is where cheap trenches fail

A trench backfilled in one loose dump will settle into a visible dip across the yard within months, and a pipe bedded on rocks instead of sand can crack under load. Proper backfill goes in thin layers, each one compacted, with clean bedding under and around the pipe. When you compare quotes, ask how the backfill is done — it is the cheapest place for a contractor to cut corners and the most expensive place for you to fix later.

Trenching jobs this guide covers

  • Water line trenches — from the meter, well, or street connection to the house, usually two and a half to three feet deep.
  • Sewer line trenches — from the house to the street or septic system, graded to a steady downhill slope.
  • Electric conduit trenches — for service runs, workshops, gates, pool equipment, and landscape lighting.
  • Septic drainfield trenches — shallow, level trenches for drain lines, dug to the dimensions on the septic design.
  • Irrigation and downspout trenches — shallow runs for sprinkler mains and buried downspout drains.

Questions to ask before you hire a trenching contractor

  • Who handles the Florida 811 locates, and will you wait for the marks before digging?
  • How deep will the trench be, and what slope will the pipe hold?
  • What bedding goes under the pipe, and how is the backfill compacted?
  • Does the quote include the pipe and connections, or digging only?
  • What happens if the trench hits groundwater — is pumping included?
  • Is surface restoration (sod, driveway patch, landscaping) included or extra?

Local tip: plan the route around the power lines

Many Lehigh Acres lots have overhead power lines near the trench route, and LCEC serves most of the area. Work near lines needs coordination with the electric provider, and some jobs can only be done by crews cleared to work near power. Mention any overhead lines in your quote request so the contractor plans for them from the start.
