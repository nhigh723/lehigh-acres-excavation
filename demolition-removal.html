Demolition & Removal in Lehigh Acres, FL

Demolition and removal clear away what's in the way — old concrete slabs, cracked driveways, worn-out sheds, and abandoned foundations — and haul the debris off your lot. Concrete removal typically runs $2–$6 per square foot, and most residential demo jobs are priced per job.

What demolition and removal covers

Lehigh Acres has plenty of lots with something old sitting on them — a cracked driveway from the 1980s, a concrete patio that has heaved and sunk, a shed that survived two hurricanes and shouldn't survive a third, or the foundation of a structure that was never finished. Demolition is the work of taking those things out: an excavator with a breaker attachment cracks concrete into manageable pieces, a grapple pulls apart wood and metal, and a dump truck hauls it all away. The ground underneath is then backfilled and rough-graded, so the spot is ready for whatever comes next — a new driveway, a patio, a garden, or nothing at all.

The work is heavier than it looks. A two-car concrete driveway four inches thick weighs roughly 20 tons, and every ton has to be broken, loaded, hauled, and disposed of. Reinforced concrete — with rebar or wire mesh inside — takes longer to break and costs more to remove than plain concrete. That weight is why hauling and disposal are usually the largest part of a demolition bill, and why a quote that only covers the breaking is only quoting half the job.

What gets removed

  • Concrete driveways and slabs — cracked, settled, or undersized concrete, broken up and hauled away.
  • Patios and sidewalks — old flatwork that has heaved, cracked, or outlived its use.
  • Sheds and small outbuildings — wood, metal, and composite structures taken down and removed.
  • Old foundations and footings — abandoned concrete footings and foundation walls dug out and hauled off.
  • Pool removal — old pools broken up, filled, and compacted so the ground is usable again.
  • Storm debris and dumped material — concrete, rubble, and construction debris cleared from a lot.

Permits and older structures

Demolition in Lee County usually needs a permit, and structures built before the 1980s may need testing for hazardous materials before they can be torn down. Utilities serving the structure — water, electric, gas — have to be disconnected first. A reputable contractor handles the permit, the disconnects, and any required testing as part of the job. Ask whether all three are included in the quote.

What happens after the teardown

Removal isn't finished when the last truck leaves. The hole left behind — where a slab sat, or where footings came out — has to be backfilled with clean fill and compacted, or it will sink into a dip within a season. The area is then rough-graded to match the surrounding ground and drain properly. If something new is going in the same spot, the backfill and compaction matter even more: a new slab poured over loose, uncompacted fill will crack. When you compare quotes, check whether backfill, compaction, and grading are included — a quote that ends at "hauled away" leaves you paying for the second half of the job separately.

Questions to ask before you hire a demolition contractor

  • Does the quote cover breaking, hauling, disposal, and cleanup — or only part of it?
  • Is backfill, compaction, and grading after removal included?
  • Who handles the Lee County demolition permit?
  • Are utilities to the structure disconnected, and who arranges that?
  • Does the structure need hazardous-material testing before teardown?
  • How do you handle Florida 811 locates before digging out footings?

Local tip: hurricane damage counts too

Hurricane season runs June through November, and Lehigh Acres has seen its share of storm-damaged structures — sheds torn apart, slabs undermined by flooding, fences and outbuildings wrecked. After a storm, demolition crews book out fast. If you have damaged structures to remove, getting a quote before the next season starts is cheaper and calmer than joining the post-storm queue.
