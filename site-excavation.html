Site Excavation in Lehigh Acres, FL

Site excavation is the earthmoving that turns a raw lot into a buildable one — cutting high ground, filling low ground, and shaping a level surface. Home site excavation typically runs $1,500–$10,000, depending on the footprint, the depth, and how much dirt has to leave the property.

What site excavation covers

Before anything gets built on a lot, the ground has to be shaped to the right height and the right slope. Site excavation is that shaping work: an excavator and a dozer move earth around the lot, cutting down high spots and filling low ones until the building area sits at the elevation the plans call for. On a new-home lot in Lehigh Acres, that usually means building up a compacted pad where the house will sit, cutting a driveway approach, and rough-grading the rest of the lot so water drains away from the structure instead of toward it.

It is distinct from the other digging on a project. Foundation digging comes after site excavation and cuts the precise trenches and pits the concrete goes into. Drainage and grading is the fine work that sets the final slopes. Site excavation is the rough, large-volume phase — the one that moves the most dirt and sets up everything that follows.

Cut and fill: the balance that decides your bill

Every site excavation is a cut-and-fill problem. Dirt cut from the high parts of the lot can be used to fill the low parts, and when the two roughly balance, very little material needs to leave the site — which keeps costs down. When a lot sits low overall, as many Lehigh Acres lots do, the balance tips the other way: fill has to be trucked in, sometimes many loads of it, and fill is billed by the cubic yard plus delivery. Fill dirt typically runs $6–$17 per cubic yard before spreading, and a pad that needs a foot of fill across a 2,000-square-foot footprint takes roughly 75 cubic yards before compaction is even factored in.

When the balance tips the opposite way — more dirt coming out than the lot can absorb — the excess has to be hauled off, and hauling and disposal typically add $8–$25 per cubic yard on top of the digging. A quote that doesn't show cut, fill, and haul as separate lines is hiding the parts of the job where the real money sits.

Water is the wildcard on Lehigh Acres sites

Groundwater sits close to the surface across much of Lehigh Acres, so a site dig that goes more than a few feet down can start filling with water. When that happens the crew pumps the water out — dewatering — to keep the excavation workable. Dewatering is routine here, but it means pumps, hoses, and time, and it belongs in the quote as its own line rather than appearing as a surprise mid-job.

What a site excavation usually includes

  • Clearing the dig area — vegetation, stumps, and debris removed from the ground to be shaped.
  • Cutting and filling to rough grade — the lot shaped to the elevations on the site plan, with the building pad compacted in lifts.
  • Driveway and access cuts — the approach from the road cut to grade, ready for a base layer.
  • Temporary drainage — swales or ditches cut so the open site sheds rain instead of ponding during construction.
  • Spoil management — excess dirt either spread on site where the plan allows or hauled away.

Questions to ask before you hire a site excavator

  • Is the quote per cubic yard, per job, or hourly — and what exactly is included?
  • How much cut, how much fill, and how much haul-off does the quote assume?
  • Is fill priced separately, and how many cubic yards does the pad need?
  • What happens if the dig hits groundwater — is dewatering included?
  • Who handles the Lee County permit and the Florida 811 locates?
  • How is the pad compacted, and is compaction testing included?

Local tip: check the lot against the road

Many Lehigh Acres lots sit below road level, and the building pad has to come up to meet it. Before you get quotes, note how much lower the lot is than the road — every inch of difference across the building footprint is fill you will pay for, and a contractor who sees it on a site visit will quote far more accurately than one guessing from a form.
